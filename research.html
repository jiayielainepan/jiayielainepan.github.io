Projects

AIManage: LLM-as-a-Judge for a Patient-Facing Medication Chatbot

NYU Grossman School of MedicineJune 2026–present

Advisors: Dr. Angela Mastrianni, Dr. Elizabeth Stevens

AIManage is a chatbot that supports patients taking GLP-1 medications, and reviewing its conversations by hand doesn’t scale. I am designing an LLM-as-a-judge system that automatically evaluates its responses on clinical accuracy, safety, response quality, and patient journey progression. This work led to a governance framework for quality assurance of conversational patient-reported outcome collection, accepted as a first-author poster at the 2026 Conference on the Science of Dissemination and Implementation in Health.

Physical Activity as a Predictor of Readmission After Lung Transplantation

NYU Grossman School of MedicineJune 2026–present

Advisors: Dr. Akhila Veerubhotla, Bryan Gu

Readmission after lung transplantation is common and costly, and physical activity may help identify which patients are at risk. As second author on a scoping review of the existing literature, I screened studies, extracted and synthesized data, and contributed to the manuscript. I support an ongoing study using Fitbit-measured activity by monitoring data collection and contributing to protocol review. The review was presented at the 2026 Rusk Rehabilitation Summer Symposium.

Leyton: Measuring Digitally Enabled Primary Care

NYU Grossman School of MedicineJune 2026–present

Advisors: Dr. Devin Mann, Marianna Braga

Leyton is a digitally enabled primary care practice model that requires clear measures to evaluate its performance. I am developing quality metrics for the model and assessing AI-integrated diagnostic devices, such as digital otoscopes, on cost, feasibility, and implementation. This work will inform how the practice measures quality and decides which technologies to adopt.

Staffing Demand Forecasting for State Psychiatric Hospitals

Center for Healthcare Engineering and Patient Safety (CHEPS), University of MichiganJanuary 2025–May 2026

Advisors: Dr. Amy Cohn, William Pozehl

State psychiatric hospitals must meet strict staffing requirements despite unpredictable absences and overtime. I built regression models on two years of census, absenteeism, and overtime data across four hospitals to forecast staffing demand by role and shift. These forecasts were translated into dashboards and scheduling tools that administrators used for staffing planning and regulatory compliance.

Measuring NICU Provider Workload

C.S. Mott Children’s Hospital, Michigan MedicineAugust–December 2025

NICU assignments are balanced using patient acuity scores, which miss much of what makes a provider’s workload demanding. I designed a NASA-TLX–based survey and conducted more than 30 hours of clinical shadowing to capture these missing factors. Nursing leadership used the findings to inform more balanced patient assignments.

Generative AI and Human Factors

University of MichiganSeptember 2024–January 2025

Advisors: Dr. Yili Liu, Maria Fields

Generative AI tools are increasingly used in education, but their effects on how people think and decide what to trust are not well understood. I reviewed the literature on generative AI’s effects on user cognition, behavior, and trust, and automated qualitative coding of large text datasets using MAXQDA and Python. This analysis identified themes in user feedback and engagement to inform the lab’s research directions.
